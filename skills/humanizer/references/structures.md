# Structures to break

Prefer stating the point directly. Restructure over synonym swap.

Sources: WriteHuman 2026, [stop-slop](https://github.com/hardikpandya/stop-slop).

---

## Binary contrasts

False drama. State Y directly; drop the negation.

| Pattern | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Not because X. Because Y. | Y is the reason. |
| X isn't the problem. Y is. | The problem is Y. |
| The answer isn't X. It's Y. | Y. |
| It feels like X. It's actually Y. | Y. |
| The question isn't X. It's Y. | Ask Y. |
| Not X. But Y. / isn't X, it's Y | Y. |
| stops being X and starts being Y | Name what changed. |
| doesn't mean X, but actually Y | State Y. |
| not just X but also Y | X and Y, or just Y if X is filler |
| Not just X, but Y / No X. No Y. Just Z. | Say it straight |

---

## Negative listing

Listing what something is *not* before revealing what it *is*.

- Not a X... Not a Y... A Z.
- It wasn't X. It wasn't Y. It was Z.

State Z. The reader doesn't need the runway.

---

## Dramatic fragmentation

Manufactured profundity.

- [Noun]. That's it. That's the [thing].
- X. And Y. And Z. (staccato drama)
- This unlocks something. [Word].
- Not always. Not perfectly. (hedging disguised as reassurance)

Complete sentences. One flat sentence is fine. Don't force a fragment every paragraph.

---

## Rhetorical setups

Announce insight instead of delivering it.

- What if [reframe]?
- Here's what I mean: / Think about it: / And that's okay.
- By the time X, I was Y. (narrative template)
- X that isn't Y → say "X is broken" or name the fix

Make the point. Let readers draw conclusions.

---

## Role-in-shaping and formulaic arcs

- X plays a crucial/critical/important role in shaping Y → say what X does
- Rule of three everywhere → two items, four, or one
- From A to B when there's no real range → name A and B
- Trailing ", highlighting...", ", ensuring...", ", fostering..."
- Every section same length; intro mirrors conclusion; topic sentence + three even sentences + closer
- Questions answered immediately in the next sentence → let questions breathe or cut them
- Every paragraph ends with a punchy one-liner → vary endings

---

## False agency

Inanimate things doing human verbs. AI uses this to avoid naming the actor.

| Pattern | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| a complaint becomes a fix | Someone fixed it that week |
| a bet lives or dies in days | Someone kills the project or ships it |
| the decision emerges | Someone decided |
| the culture shifts | People change behavior |
| the conversation moves toward | Someone steers |
| the data tells us | Someone read it and concluded |
| the market rewards | Buyers pay for things |

Name the human. If no specific person fits, use "you" to put the reader in the seat.

---

## Narrator-from-a-distance

Floating above the scene instead of putting the reader in it.

- Nobody designed this. → You don't sit down one day and decide to...
- This happens because... / This is why... → Name the mechanism or actor
- People tend to... → You probably... or name the group

Put the reader in the room. "You" beats "People." Specifics beat abstractions.

---

## Passive voice

Hides the actor. Find who did it; put them at the front.

- X was created → [Person] created X
- It is believed that → [Group] believes
- Mistakes were made → [Who] made them
- The decision was reached → [Who] decided

Genre note: passive is OK in lab reports, some legal prose, and when the actor is genuinely unknown. Default to active elsewhere.

---

## Sentence starters to avoid

| Pattern | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Sentences starting with What, When, Where, Which, Who, Why, How | Lead with subject or verb |
| Paragraphs starting with So | Start with content |
| Sentences starting with Look, | Remove |

"What makes this hard is..." → name the specific constraint.

---

## Rhythm and punctuation

- Three consecutive sentences within ~5 words of the same length → split one or merge two; do this across the piece
- Three-item lists when two or one would do
- Em dashes: default cut. Period, comma, or colon. Rare em dash for a real interruption (~1 per 400 words max)
- En dashes for ranges (1990–2000)
- Lazy extremes (every, always, never, everyone, nobody) → specifics instead of sweeping claims
- Cut quotables: if it sounds like a pull-quote or poster line ("X is where Y is won or lost"), rewrite it

---

## Bad humanizer output (its own tell)

Poster aphorisms, em dashes as default punctuation, zinger at every paragraph end, three parallel beats every time, slide-deck bullets (`**Label:** text`), every sentence the same length on purpose, thesaurus voice, designed messiness, engagement bait swapped for bot bait.

If it sounds "humanized," strip polish until it's boring and clear.
